No universal absence, order or number of internal callbacks is asserted.

The expected result uses only literal `Long` coefficients and scales, primitive multiplication/division/remainder, and character formatting. It calls no production `BigDecimal` or `BigInteger` arithmetic and no JDK numerical provider. For input pairs `(A,sa)` and `(B,sb)`, let `S = sa-sb`. The exact rational value is `A/B × 10^-S`; the integer part toward zero is computed as `(A × 10^max(0,-S)) / (B × 10^max(0,S))`. Kotlin signed `Long` division truncates toward zero, matching the required integral operation, including a negative divisor and negative dividends.

For integer part `K`, zero has coefficient zero and preferred scale `S`. For nonzero `K` and `S >= 0`, the required coefficient is `K × 10^S` at scale `S`. For `S < 0`, remove trailing decimal zeros from `K` only until scale reaches `S`, or until a nonzero last digit prevents further removal. This is exactly the closest-to-preferred representable integer cohort: a negative scale requires divisibility by the corresponding power of ten. No optional cohort rescue, endpoint clamping or arbitrary scale stripping is introduced. Unlimited precision imposes no additional significant-digit rounding.
